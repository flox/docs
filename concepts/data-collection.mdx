## Summary

**What does Flox collect?**
The Flox CLI records telemetry about every command you run, with your search terms, package names and environment names.
[Telemetry fields](#telemetry-fields) lists every field.
Flox from Flox's installers, Homebrew and `flox/flox` container images also sends error reports and performance traces with your device ID and file paths to Sentry.
FloxHub logs your IP address with every request the CLI sends it.
When you push an environment or create one on FloxHub, FloxHub stores its manifest, lockfile and a history entry with your login name, hostname and `flox` command line.
FloxHub makes these environments public by default.
Your FloxHub account, the install script and the FloxHub web app also collect data.
[Data categories](#data-categories) lists every category.

**Is it linked to me?**
Yes.
Every telemetry event carries a random device ID that Flox stores on your machine.
When you're signed in, telemetry events also carry your FloxHub account ID.
Flox's analytics data joins that account ID to your email address.
Flox links your device ID to every account that signs in on that device.
Flox also links your device ID to your IP address.
[CLI telemetry](#cli-telemetry) lists every identifier.

**Can I turn it off?**
Yes.
Set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` or `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` in your shell profile before your first Flox command.
This turns off CLI telemetry, error reports, performance traces and the device ID.
[Turn off telemetry](#turn-off-telemetry) lists every setting.

**What can't I turn off?**
You can't turn off the requests that search, show, resolve or publish packages, or use FloxHub environments.
You can't turn off FloxHub's records of those requests, which contain your IP address and, when you're signed in, your handle and account ID.
You can't turn off the history FloxHub stores when you push an environment.
You can't turn off the background upgrade check that `flox activate` starts, which sends the environment's package list to the catalog when you're signed in.
A [self-hosted FloxHub](#self-hosted-floxhub) keeps all four on your own servers.
[What turning it off does not stop](#what-turning-it-off-does-not-stop) lists every item.

**How long is it kept?**
Flox keeps CLI telemetry indefinitely.
FloxHub keeps catalog request records, with your IP address and search terms, indefinitely.
Flox doesn't delete either automatically.
Flox keeps the manifests and lockfiles you push indefinitely, also after you delete the environment.
[Retention](#retention) lists every store.

**What never leaves my machine?**
Your source files, the commands you run in an environment and your log files stay on your machine.
Your manifest's `[vars]`, `[hook]`, `[profile]`, `[services]` and `[build]` sections stay on your machine until you push the environment.
[What never leaves your machine](#what-never-leaves-your-machine) lists every item and its exceptions.

## What never leaves your machine

| Stays on your machine | Exceptions |
| --- | --- |
| Your source files and other project files | `flox push` uploads `manifest.toml` and `manifest.lock`. `flox publish` uploads the package your build produces, which contains source files only if your build copies them into it. `flox publish` also sends your git remote URL and revision. `flox build`, `flox develop` and `flox publish` send the names of the catalog packages that `.flox/pkgs/*.nix` refers to. `flox init` reads the Node.js, Python or Go version your project asks for and sends that version constraint, with the names of the Flox catalog packages it picks, to the catalog. |
| Your manifest's `[vars]` values and its `[hook]`, `[profile]`, `[services]`, `[build]`, `[include]` and `[containerize]` sections | `flox push`, `flox init -r` and creating a default environment upload the whole manifest. Telemetry from `flox build` contains a hash of your lockfile, and the lockfile contains the whole manifest. |
| The values of your environment variables | Telemetry contains the value of `FLOX_INVOCATION_SOURCE` as you set it, and your shell's name. Push history contains your login name. Your FloxHub token goes to FloxHub as your credential, and to Flox's package store when Nix downloads or uploads custom catalog packages. Requests to FloxHub environment storage carry the language of your locale. Performance traces contain your home directory's path inside the file paths they record. |
| Commands you run with `flox activate -c`, after `flox activate --`, and the arguments you pass to `flox run` | Push history contains each `flox` command line in full. `flox run` and `flox search --command` send the command name to the catalog. When a crash message quotes a command-line argument, the error report contains that argument. |
| Error message text | Error reports contain crash messages and the messages Flox printed or logged before the error. Telemetry contains only a fixed error category. |
| Activation state, the activation trace and the shell scripts Flox generates | Error reports from the activation process contain the output of your `hook.on-deactivate` script. |
| Log files in `.flox/log/` and `~/.cache/flox/log/` | None. |
| Your list of environments and their paths, auto-activation and trust choices, and other local state files | Performance traces contain the absolute paths of your environment directories, of the directory you pass to `flox pull`, of Flox's data directory, and of the files a step reads or writes. |
| The fingerprint of your FloxHub token that Flox caches | None. |
| Your package signing key | `flox publish` uploads only the signatures. |

No CLI request body contains an IP address field.
Every server you connect to sees your IP address.
FloxHub records your IP address with each catalog request.
With telemetry on, that record also contains your device ID.

### CLI telemetry

Flox sends telemetry to `api.flox.dev/events`.
Every event carries your device ID.
Events carry your account ID when you're signed in.
[Telemetry fields](#telemetry-fields) lists every field.

Flox saves each command's events to a file on your machine.
When a command finishes and the oldest saved event is more than 2 minutes old, Flox starts `flox send-telemetry` in the background.
Your command exits without waiting for it.
`flox send-telemetry` sends every saved event when the oldest one is more than 2 minutes old, 100 per request.
`flox activate` and `flox develop` send due events before they start your shell.
Flox keeps fewer than 1,000 saved events, and drops the oldest.
Interactive and in-place activations install the shell prompt hook (`flox hook-env`).
The hook records two events at every prompt, and on tcsh also at every directory change.
When your shell startup file activates an environment in place, for example with `eval "$(flox activate -D)"` for [auto-activation](/concepts/auto-activation), the hook runs in every shell you open.

The first Flox run with telemetry on that can print a notice creates the device ID and prints the notice.
Runs with `-q`, runs with a `RUST_LOG` value that hides Flox's messages, shell completion, the background upgrade check and `flox send-telemetry` can't print it.
While `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` doesn't exist, a run that can't print the notice creates no device ID, saves no events, and sends no CLI telemetry, error reports or performance traces.
Such a run also doesn't look up the account ID of a personal access token or service account token when it starts.
Requests from such a run carry no `flox-device-uuid`, `flox-invocation-id` or `sentry-trace` header.
Whenever `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` is missing, the next run that prints the notice creates a new device ID.

Your account ID identifies your FloxHub account.
After `flox auth login`, it is your sign-in provider and your user ID there, for example `github|1234567`.
For a GitHub sign-in, it contains your numeric GitHub user ID.
For a sign-in through your company's SAML SSO, it contains the user ID your identity provider sends.
When your identity provider sends your email address as that user ID, your account ID contains your email address.
Flox keeps your account ID indefinitely in CLI telemetry, catalog request records and its copies of Auth0 events.
When your account ID contains your email address, those records keep your email address indefinitely.
For a personal access token, it is the account ID of the person who created the token.
For a service account token, it is the service account's ID.
When telemetry is on and you use a personal access token or service account token, Flox looks up the account ID on FloxHub when a command starts.
Flox caches the result for a token it stores.
When the token is in `FLOX_FLOXHUB_TOKEN`, Flox repeats the lookup in every Flox process except the shell prompt hook, including the background upgrade check and `flox send-telemetry`.
If the lookup fails or takes longer than 2 seconds, that run's events carry no account ID.
The shell prompt hook's events carry your account ID only when the token from `flox auth login` is in `FLOX_FLOXHUB_TOKEN` or in your `flox.toml`.
They carry no account ID when that token is in your system keychain, or when you use a personal access token or service account token.

| Identifier | Stored in | Sent in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device ID, a random UUID | `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` | Every telemetry event, every error report and performance trace, the `flox-device-uuid` header on FloxHub requests, installer telemetry |
| Invocation ID, a random UUID for each run of `flox` | Memory | Every telemetry event, the `flox-invocation-id` header on catalog and account requests |
| Local environment ID, a random UUID for each local environment | `env_id` in `.flox/env.json`, which you commit with your project | Telemetry events about that environment |
| Account ID | Your credential; cached in `~/.cache/flox/auth-state-*.json` | `auth_subject` on every telemetry event when Flox knows it |

| File | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` | Your device ID |
| `~/.local/share/flox/events-v2.json` | Saved telemetry events, including your account ID |
| `~/.local/share/flox/events-v2.lock` | Empty lock file |
| `~/.cache/flox/metrics-lock` | Empty lock file |
| `~/.cache/flox/log/send-telemetry.log` | The log of the last `flox send-telemetry` run. It contains the invocation ID and the FloxHub API URL. |
| `.flox/log/upgrade-check.*.log` in each environment, and in `~/.cache/flox/remote/*/*/` for environments you use with `-r` | The log of the background upgrade check. With telemetry on, the git commands it records for FloxHub environments contain your device ID. |

### Error and performance reports

Flox packages built by Flox's installers send error reports and performance traces to Sentry at `*.ingest.us.sentry.io`.
These packages are:

- the macOS package, which Homebrew also installs
- the Linux deb and rpm packages
- the Flox that the get.flox.dev install script installs
- the `flox/flox` container images

The GitHub Action and the CircleCI orb install these packages unless you use their Nix install route.
Imageless Kubernetes nodes install these packages.
Flox built from the Flox flake or installed by the NixOS, nix-darwin or home-manager modules sends no error reports.

Every error report and performance trace contains:

- your device ID, as the Sentry user ID
- the Flox version and its release channel

Earlier Flox releases also send your computer's hostname with every error report and performance trace.

Each run of a Flox command sends one performance trace, with these exceptions:

- `flox activate` sends one trace from its background upgrade check, one when the activation ends, and one for each locking, building or FloxHub step it performs.
- `flox run` and `flox develop` send one trace for each resolving, downloading or building step they perform.
- `flox build` and `flox publish` send a trace only when they find an environment.
- `flox` with no command, `flox deactivate`, `flox activate allow`, `flox activate deny`, `flox help`, the shell prompt hook, Flox's internal `activation-state`, `services-socket` and `send-telemetry` commands, `--help` and `--version` send none.

A performance trace contains the name, timing, and Flox source file and line of each step, and the values that step works on: search terms, your system, package names and paths, flake references, Nix store paths, git revisions, environment names, `owner/name`, FloxHub and git URLs, branch names, your invocation sources, container names and tags, `flox gc` progress output, and the absolute paths of your environment directories, of Flox's data directory, and of the files a step reads or writes.

These commands set tags:

| Command | Tags |
| --- | --- |
| `flox search` | `search_term`, `json`, `show_all` |
| `flox show` | `pkg_path` |
| `flox install`, `flox uninstall` | `packages` |
| `flox list` | `list_mode` |

An error report carries a tag only when Flox records the error inside the step that set the tag, before that step pauses or ends.
Performance traces don't carry these tags.
The `packages` tag lists every package you named in `flox install`, or the arguments you passed to `flox uninstall`.

Flox sends an error report when the CLI or the activation process crashes or logs an error, and when the update check fails for a reason other than a connection failure or timeout.
An error report contains:

- the error or crash message
- the stack trace, with source file paths
- the absolute path of every library loaded into the process, including the `flox` binary
- your operating system's name, version, build and kernel version
- your Mac's model, on macOS
- your CPU architecture and the Rust version
- up to 100 messages Flox printed or logged earlier in that run, each with the values of the steps it ran in, including messages that `-q` hides and output lines from the container build in `flox containerize`, except messages logged inside a step that paused or ended before the error
- the tags listed above, for an error inside the step that set them
- for the activation process, the output of your `hook.on-deactivate` script
- for an update check that gets an error status, the response body from `downloads.flox.dev`

Flox sends Sentry no account ID and no IP address field.
Sentry sees your IP address from the connection.
Whether Sentry stores that IP address, or a location it derives from it, with your reports: TBD {/* OPEN: in Sentry, open a recent CLI error report and a recent CLI performance trace and read user.ip_address and user.geo (city, region, country); then read Settings → Projects → each CLI and activation project → Security & Privacy → Prevent Storing of IP Addresses. Sentry derives a location even when that setting is on. If either field is set, state what Sentry stores here and in the Data categories row for error and performance reports. */}
`disable_metrics` and `DO_NOT_TRACK` are the only settings that turn off error reports.

### Catalog and FloxHub requests

Flox sends a request to the catalog or FloxHub whenever a feature needs one.
These requests carry these headers:

| Header | Value | Sent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `user-agent` | `flox-cli/` and your Flox version | Catalog, account, git and sign-in requests |
| `authorization` | Your FloxHub token | When you're signed in |
| `flox-invocation-source` | The labels of the tools that started Flox, and each value of `FLOX_INVOCATION_SOURCE` | When Flox detects a tool, also with telemetry off |
| `flox-device-uuid` | Your device ID | Catalog, account and git requests, when telemetry is on |
| `flox-invocation-id` | The invocation ID | Catalog and account requests, when telemetry is on |
| `sentry-trace` | The Sentry trace ID | Catalog requests, when error reports are on |
| `api-version` | `unknown` | Catalog requests |
| `x-flox-qos-context` | `background` | Requests from the background upgrade check |
| `accept-language` | The language of your locale | Git requests |

Each request sends this content:

| Request | Sent by | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resolve packages | `flox install` of a new catalog package, `flox upgrade`, `flox run`, `flox init` language detection, `flox pull` adding your system, and any command that locks a missing lockfile or a new or changed catalog package or system, including `flox edit`, `flox uninstall` and `flox include upgrade`; the background upgrade check when you're signed in | For each package and system: the package group name, install ID, package path, system, version constraint, allow-broken, allow-unfree and allow-pre-release settings, allowed licenses, and the derivation path locked before. Flox merges included environments first. |
| Search | `flox search`; suggestions after a failed `flox install`; `flox init` for Node.js | The search term without its `@version`, your system, the page size |
| Show | `flox show` | The package path |
| Find a package by command | `flox run` without a saved choice; `flox search --command` | The command name as you typed it, including any path, and your system |
| Store information | Builds and runs that need custom catalog packages; `flox publish` | Nix store paths |
| Base catalog information | Nix expression builds; `flox publish` | Nothing |
| Build input lookup | `flox build`, `flox develop` and `flox publish` without a committed `.flox/catalog.lock` | The catalog package references in `.flox/pkgs/*.nix` |
| Publish | `flox publish` | Your git remote URL, revision, revision count and date, branch, the `.flox` path inside your repository, the Nixpkgs revision, the package's derivation path, name, version, description, license, outputs, system, and broken and unfree flags, the locked base catalog and inputs, the store the package uploads to, Nix metadata for every store path in the package's closure, the build type, whether you passed `--allow-lineage-change`, and the built package |
| Account lookup | Commands that use a personal access token or service account token | Your token |
| Environment storage (git over HTTPS) | `flox push`, `flox pull`, `-r` commands, first use of a FloxHub environment, `flox list --upstream`, `flox include upgrade`, locking an environment that includes a FloxHub environment, the background upgrade check | Git requests; `flox push` uploads what [Environments you push](#environments-you-push) lists |
| Sign-in | `flox auth login` | The client ID of the Flox CLI, the scopes `openid profile email`, and the device code |

For each catalog request it accepts, FloxHub records:

- the route, path, query string including search terms, method, status, duration and error class
- every header except credential headers, including your IP address, user agent, device ID, invocation ID and invocation sources
- your handle and account ID, and your credential type, when you're signed in
- the organization ID, for a service account token
- for a resolve request, the package group names, package paths, version constraints and systems you asked for, and the versions, catalogs and systems FloxHub returned
- for a store request, the Nix store paths
- for a search, the number of results

FloxHub's git service logs your handle, the repository, whether you read or wrote, and the commit IDs you push.
FloxHub's git service also logs the time, path, status and user agent of each git request.
FloxHub's API gateway logs your IP address, the time, the route and the status of every request.
FloxHub's services log the path and query string of each request, and your handle when you're signed in.
FloxHub's own error monitoring in Sentry receives your IP address, user agent, device ID header and query string with every request.
It also receives the search term, command name, package path or catalog the request works on.
When FloxHub checks your access to a catalog or environment, its error monitoring receives your account ID and the catalog name or the environment's `owner/name`.
When FloxHub denies you access, its error monitoring receives your account ID and handle.
When you publish a package, its error monitoring receives your handle.
When you download a custom catalog package from a Flox package store, the store logs your IP address, your FloxHub handle and the store path.
When Flox issues storage credentials for a custom catalog download or a publish, Flox's cloud audit log records your FloxHub handle.

### Environments you push

`flox push`, `flox init -r` and creating a default environment upload:

| Item | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `manifest.toml` | Your whole manifest, for every generation, including `[vars]` values, hooks, profile scripts, service commands and build commands |
| `manifest.lock` | Your lockfile for every generation, which contains the whole manifest again |
| History entry | The operation, the packages it changed, your login name, your hostname, the full `flox` command line, the time, and the generation numbers |
| Commit dates | Your time zone offset |

Earlier generations keep content you later remove from the manifest.
FloxHub makes environments you push public by default.
Anyone, signed in or not, can pull a public environment and read its manifest, lockfile and history.
Anyone who can pull an environment, public or private, can read its history, including the login names, hostnames and command lines in it.
`flox push` doesn't upload `.flox/env.json`, `.flox/catalog.lock`, `.flox/pkgs/`, logs or other project files.

### Account data

Flox uses Auth0 for sign-in.
Auth0 stores the profile your sign-in provider (GitHub, Google, GitLab or your company's SSO) returns:

- your user ID at that provider
- your email address and whether it's verified
- your name, given name, family name, nickname and profile picture URL
- your username and phone number, if the provider returns them

Flox sets the nickname to your FloxHub handle.
Auth0 also records when you signed up and last signed in, your last sign-in IP address, and how many times you signed in.
Auth0 logs each sign-in with your IP address, user agent, user ID, user name and location.

Every 15 minutes, Flox copies these events from Auth0's logs into its own analytics data: sign-up, sign-in, sign-out, a change to your email address, username or password, and Flox deleting your account.
Each copy contains Auth0's log ID and event type, your account ID, the time, the sign-in connection, the client and client library, the requested scopes and API, the session and transaction IDs, the organization ID, Auth0's description of the event, and the name, environment and hostname of Flox's Auth0 tenant.
Flox doesn't copy your IP address, user agent, user name or location, and replaces email addresses and IP addresses in the description with a marker.
Flox also copies any new single-value field Auth0 adds to these logs, unless its value contains an email address or IP address.
When FloxHub adds you to an organization, Flox copies that event with your account ID, the organization ID, the time and the ID of the client that added you.
Flox keeps these copies indefinitely, also after it deletes your account.
If your account ID contains your email address, these copies contain it too.

Flox stores your handle, the client you signed up with, whether you completed sign-up and whether Flox recorded it, whether and when you finished onboarding, and your HubSpot contact ID with your account.
From the profile form, Flox stores your company, your newsletter choice and how you heard about Flox.

At your first sign-in, from the web or the CLI, Flox sends a "Signed Up" event to Amplitude.
The event contains your email address, or your account ID if you have no email address, the sign-up client, and the sign-in connection and its type.
When you sign up from the CLI, Flox creates a HubSpot contact with your email address, a FloxHub sign-up flag, and the sign-up client `Flox CLI`.
When you complete your profile on the web, Flox sends HubSpot your email address, company, newsletter choice, how you heard about Flox, sign-up client, and whether you used a mobile device.
When you edit your profile on the web, Flox sends HubSpot your email address, company and newsletter choice.
If Flox has no HubSpot contact ID for you, that edit also sends how you heard about Flox and your sign-up client.

### Installer telemetry

The install script at get.flox.dev sends two events to Flox each time it runs.

| Event | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| `installer.started` | Operating system, CPU architecture, channel, version, whether Nix is installed, whether Flox is installed |
| `installer.completed` | Operating system, CPU architecture, channel, version, outcome, install path, failure reason |

Both events also carry a random event ID, the time, a random ID for the install run, and your device ID if `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` exists.
Set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` for the script to turn these events off; see [Install script](#install-script).

The sign-in pages at auth.flox.dev, including the page `flox auth login` opens, load HubSpot and Google Tag Manager.
Flox CLI settings don't affect them.

## Turn off telemetry

`disable_metrics = true` turns off, in the CLI and every process it starts:

- CLI telemetry
- error reports and performance traces, including those from the background upgrade check and the activation process
- the device ID: Flox doesn't create `metrics-uuid` and doesn't print the first-run notice
- the account ID lookup for tokens
- the `flox-device-uuid`, `flox-invocation-id` and `sentry-trace` headers on requests to FloxHub

The `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS` environment variable accepts only `true` or `false`, in any letter case.
In a `flox.toml` file and with `flox config --set`, write lowercase `true` or `false` without quotes.
Any other value, for example `1`, `yes` or an empty value, makes Flox commands exit with an error.
That run creates no device ID and sends no telemetry, error reports or performance traces.
For `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=1`, Flox prints:

```text
✘ ERROR: Could not parse config: invalid type: integer `1`, expected a boolean
```

Flox reads the setting from these places, and each later place overrides the earlier ones:

1. The system file `/etc/flox.toml`, or `flox.toml` in the directory that `FLOX_SYSTEM_CONFIG_DIR` names.
2. Your user configuration file. Flox uses `flox.toml` in the directory that `FLOX_CONFIG_DIR` names, or else the first file that exists of `~/.config/flox/flox.toml` and `/etc/xdg/flox/flox.toml`. `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` and `XDG_CONFIG_DIRS` change those two locations.
3. The `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS` environment variable.

When `DO_NOT_TRACK` turns telemetry off, it overrides all three places.

Turn telemetry off before you run Flox for the first time, so that Flox never creates a device ID.

### Environment variable

```bash
export FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true
```

Add this line to your shell profile to keep the setting.
When telemetry is off, Flox sets `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` for every process it starts, including your activated shell.

### `DO_NOT_TRACK`

```bash
export DO_NOT_TRACK=1
```

Flox also reads the `DO_NOT_TRACK` environment variable.
Any value other than an empty value, `0` or `false` turns off everything that `disable_metrics = true` turns off.
Flox ignores letter case and surrounding whitespace in the value.
`DO_NOT_TRACK` overrides `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=false`, and `disable_metrics = false` in every configuration file.
While `DO_NOT_TRACK` turns telemetry off, `flox config` shows `disable_metrics = true`.
An empty value, `0` or `false` leaves the other settings in effect.
The install script doesn't read `DO_NOT_TRACK`.

### User configuration

```bash
flox config --set disable_metrics true
```

This command writes `disable_metrics = true` to your user configuration file, `~/.config/flox/flox.toml` by default.
If it is the first Flox command you run on the machine, the command itself creates a device ID and sends one performance trace to Sentry.
Use the environment variable for that first run to prevent this.
`flox -q config --set disable_metrics true` as the first command also creates no device ID and sends no performance trace.

### Every user on a machine

```toml title="/etc/flox.toml"
disable_metrics = true
```

Write lowercase `true` without quotes.
A quoted value makes Flox commands exit with an error.
A user's own `flox.toml` and the `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS` environment variable override `/etc/flox.toml`.
A user's own `flox.toml` doesn't override the environment variable, so also export `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` from a system-wide shell profile.
`DO_NOT_TRACK=1` in that profile also overrides a user's `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=false`.
On macOS, installing or upgrading Flox with the `.pkg` installer or Homebrew replaces `/etc/flox.toml`, so set it again after each upgrade.

A user can unset `DO_NOT_TRACK`, so Flox has no setting that a user can't override.
To enforce the setting, block the telemetry and error-report endpoints at your network edge, as [Network endpoints](#network-endpoints) describes.

### NixOS

```nix title="configuration.nix"
services.flox.metrics.enable = false;
environment.etc."flox.toml".text = "disable_metrics = true\n";
```

`services.flox.metrics.enable` applies only to the systemd services that the Flox NixOS module runs.
Its default, `true`, sets `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=false` for those services, which overrides `/etc/flox.toml`.
The module has no telemetry option for people who run Flox themselves, so the second line writes `/etc/flox.toml` for them.

### Imageless Kubernetes

```yaml
metadata:
  annotations:
    flox.dev/disable-metrics: "true"
```

The annotation sets `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` for the node's `flox pull` and `flox list` that prepare the pod's environment, for the container's `flox activate`, and for processes you start with `kubectl exec`.
It doesn't cover the node bootstrap command, `flox activate -r flox/containerd-shim-flox-installer`.
Set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` before that command in your node startup script.
See [Imageless Kubernetes configuration](/imageless-kubernetes/config#telemetry).

### CI

In GitHub Actions, set the input on the Flox install action:

```yaml
- uses: flox/install-flox-action@v2
  with:
    disable-metrics: "true"
```

The action sets `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS` for the rest of the job to the exact value you write, so write `true`.

In CircleCI, the Flox orb's `disable_metrics` parameter applies only to the orb's install step.
Set the variable on the job instead:

```yaml
jobs:
  build:
    environment:
      FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS: "true"
```

In other CI systems, set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` in the job's environment.

### Install script

```bash
curl -fsSL https://get.flox.dev | FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true sh
```

For telemetry, the install script reads only this environment variable.
It ignores `DO_NOT_TRACK`, `flox.toml` and `/etc/flox.toml`.

### Confirm telemetry is off

`flox config` prints the value Flox uses, whichever place set it:

```console
$ flox config | grep disable_metrics
disable_metrics = true
```

With `-vv`, Flox logs that telemetry is off:

```console
$ flox -vv config 2>&1 | grep 'Metrics collection disabled'
19:32:48.494 DEBUG flox::commands: Metrics collection disabled
```

On a machine where telemetry has always been off, `~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid` doesn't exist.
Turning telemetry off doesn't delete a device ID or events Flox saved earlier.
Delete them from their default locations:

```bash
rm -f ~/.local/share/flox/metrics-uuid
rm -f ~/.local/share/flox/events-v2.json ~/.local/share/flox/events-v2.lock
rm -f ~/.cache/flox/metrics-lock
```

The background upgrade check's logs also contain the device ID, in the git commands they record for FloxHub environments.
Delete `.flox/log/upgrade-check.*.log` in each environment.
For environments you used with `-r`, delete `~/.cache/flox/remote/*/*/.flox/log/upgrade-check.*.log`.

## What turning it off does not stop

| What still happens | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Requests a feature needs | Searching, resolving, showing and publishing packages, and FloxHub environment operations still reach the catalog and FloxHub, with the content [Catalog and FloxHub requests](#catalog-and-floxhub-requests) lists. |
| Request headers | Those requests still carry `user-agent` with your Flox version, `api-version`, `authorization` with your FloxHub token when you're signed in, `x-flox-qos-context` from the background upgrade check, and `flox-invocation-source` with the tool labels and your `FLOX_INVOCATION_SOURCE` value. |
| FloxHub's records | FloxHub still records each request with your IP address, search terms and package lists, and your handle and account ID when you're signed in. |
| The background upgrade check | Each `flox activate` starts `flox check-for-upgrades`. When you're signed in, and the last successful check is more than 24 hours old or the lockfile changed, the check sends the environment's package list to the catalog. If that request fails, the check runs again at the next activation. For a FloxHub environment, the check also fetches the environment from FloxHub on the same schedule, whether or not you're signed in. `upgrade_notifications = false` in your configuration, or `options.activate.upgrade-notifications = false` in the manifest, hides the notice and doesn't stop the check. |
| The update check | When you set `installer_channel` and run Flox in an interactive terminal, Flox asks `downloads.flox.dev` for the latest version at most once a day. Flox doesn't run this check unless you set `installer_channel`. |
| Local environment IDs | `flox init` and `flox pull --copy` write a random ID to `.flox/env.json`, which you commit with your project. |
| Push history | `flox push` uploads your login name, hostname and full `flox` command line with each generation. |
| Account lookups | With a personal access token or service account token, `flox push`, sign-in checks, trust checks, `flox auth status` and `flox auth login --token-file` look up your account on FloxHub. |
| Nix downloads | Nix still downloads packages from `cache.nixos.org` and any other substituters you configure, custom catalog packages from `publisher.flox.dev` and the storage hosts it returns, and sources from GitHub. Nix looks up flake references without a URL on `channels.nixos.org`. |
| Installer telemetry | The install script ignores `DO_NOT_TRACK`, `flox.toml` and `/etc/flox.toml`. Set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` for the script. |
| The Imageless Kubernetes label | Imageless Kubernetes still sends the `containerd` invocation source on FloxHub requests. |
| Running activations | An activation that started while telemetry was on keeps sending error reports and performance traces until it ends. |
| Saved data | Flox keeps the device ID and saved events. If you turn telemetry back on, Flox sends the saved events with the old device ID. |
| FloxHub web app | Flox CLI settings don't affect hub.flox.dev. |

## Self-hosted FloxHub

When you point the Flox CLI at a self-hosted FloxHub, the CLI still sends telemetry to Flox, and error reports and performance traces to Sentry, unless you turn telemetry off.
Flox's telemetry endpoint is built into the CLI, and `floxhub_url` doesn't change it.
That telemetry contains the account ID your FloxHub or your identity provider assigns, the search terms you type, and the `owner/name` of your environments.
Telemetry events don't contain your FloxHub's URL.
Performance traces from Flox's installer-built packages do contain the FloxHub URLs that each step uses.

Set `disable_metrics = true` in `/etc/flox.toml` on every machine that uses your FloxHub, and see [Turn off telemetry](#turn-off-telemetry) for the settings that override it.
With telemetry off, the CLI sends nothing to Flox's telemetry or error-report endpoints.

Serve `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource` from your FloxHub.
Without it, `flox auth login` signs in through Flox's hosted sign-in at `auth.flox.dev`.

With its default configuration, the self-hosted FloxHub server sends no telemetry or error reports to Flox, and its web app loads no analytics tools.

## Network endpoints

Flox and its tools use HTTPS on port 443 for every host except your own git remote, which uses the protocol in its URL.

| Host or URL | Purpose | When | Needed for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `api.flox.dev` | Catalog API and FloxHub account lookups | Searching, showing, resolving and publishing packages; `flox run`; the background upgrade check when you're signed in; commands that use a token | Every command that uses the catalog |
| `api.flox.dev/git` | FloxHub environment storage, a path on `api.flox.dev` | `flox push`, `flox pull`, `-r` commands, first use of a FloxHub environment, the background upgrade check for a FloxHub environment, locking an environment that includes a FloxHub environment | FloxHub environments |
| `api.flox.dev/events` | Telemetry, a path on `api.flox.dev` | When saved events are due | Nothing; it shares a hostname with the API, so blocking it needs a proxy that filters by path |
| `*.ingest.us.sentry.io` | Error reports and performance traces | Commands with a traced step, and errors | Nothing; you can block it by hostname |
| `hub.flox.dev` | FloxHub web app; sign-in discovery | `flox auth login` | `flox auth login`, which fails if the CLI can't connect |
| `auth.flox.dev` | Sign-in | `flox auth login` | Signing in |
| `cache.nixos.org` | Nix binary cache | Downloading packages that aren't on your machine | Installing and activating packages |
| `cache.flox.dev` | Flox binary cache | When it's in your Nix `substituters`, or the catalog returns it | Packages from Flox's cache |
| `publisher.flox.dev` and the storage hosts it returns | Custom catalog packages | Installing or activating custom catalog packages; `flox publish` | Custom catalogs and publishing |
| `github.com`, `codeload.github.com` | Nixpkgs sources and flake references | `flox build`, `flox develop`, `flox publish`, installing flake references, building from source, `flox containerize` on macOS | Builds |
| `channels.nixos.org` | Nix flake registry | Flake references without a URL, for example `nixpkgs#hello` | Those references |
| `downloads.flox.dev` | Update check; deb and rpm repositories | The update check, only with `installer_channel` set; package manager upgrades | Upgrading Flox |
| `get.flox.dev` | Install script | Running the install script | Installing Flox with the script |

| Your git remote | Revision check | `flox publish` | Publishing |

The install script sends installer telemetry to a separate Flox host, not to get.flox.dev.
Set `FLOX_DISABLE_METRICS=true` for the script to stop it.

With telemetry off, a CLI user on FloxHub needs `api.flox.dev`, `cache.nixos.org`, and `auth.flox.dev` and `hub.flox.dev` to sign in.
Add `cache.flox.dev` and `publisher.flox.dev` if you use them, and `github.com` and `codeload.github.com` for builds.

Requests from the Flox CLI itself honor `HTTPS_PROXY`, `HTTP_PROXY`, `ALL_PROXY` and `NO_PROXY`, and on macOS the system proxy settings.
Requests to Sentry honor `HTTPS_PROXY` and `HTTP_PROXY`, and ignore `NO_PROXY` and `ALL_PROXY`.

## Data requests and deletion

To ask for a copy of your data or to delete your account, email privacy@flox.dev.
FloxHub has no self-service account deletion.
Flox deletes your account manually after you ask.

Before you ask, delete the FloxHub environments you no longer want with the **Delete** button on FloxHub, and [revoke your personal access tokens](/concepts/personal-access-tokens#viewing-and-revoking-tokens).

Deleting your account removes your account record.
It doesn't delete:

- Auth0's sign-in logs
- the copies of Auth0 sign-in and account events in Flox's analytics data
- the daily account backups
- environments you pushed, and the manifests and lockfiles of environments you deleted
- catalogs and packages you published
- CLI telemetry and installer telemetry Flox already received
- FloxHub's records and logs of your requests
- package store download logs and the cloud audit log
- request logs for downloads.flox.dev, get.flox.dev, cache.flox.dev and hub.flox.dev
- error reports in Sentry
- the copies held by Amplitude, HubSpot, Intercom, Common Room, Vector, Stripe, Google Analytics, LinkedIn, Reddit and OpenAI Ads
- the posts about your sign-up, your marketing-email choice and your organization's billing in Flox's Slack workspace
- the organization member lists in Flox's older analytics database
- the daily copies of your email address that Flox's analytics data takes from your account record, which Flox deletes 365 days after the last daily copy
